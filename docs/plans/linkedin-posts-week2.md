## Post 1: Microsoft 365 — The Defaults Aren't Enough (Tuesday AM)

Most Australian SMBs run on Microsoft 365.

Email, Teams, SharePoint, OneDrive. It's the backbone of your business.

Here's the problem: the default settings leave you exposed.

Three things we find in almost every new client's M365 tenant:

1. No MFA on admin accounts. One password guess away from someone owning your domain, your email, your files.
2. Shared logins. "It's easier." Yes, until the person who left six weeks ago still has access to the accounts mailbox.
3. Inbox rules nobody knows about. We've found attackers sitting silently in inboxes for weeks — reading invoices, redirecting payments, learning the rhythm of the business.

The good news: locking this down takes about two hours for a tenant under 50 users.

The bad news: most businesses don't know they need to.

We check M365 config as part of every health check. Takes 15 minutes.

## Post 2: When "Cheap IT" Gets Expensive (Wednesday PM)

Every growing business hits this moment.

"We've got a guy. He's pretty good with computers."

The guy is usually decent. Might know Windows inside out. Probably charges less than we do.

Here's what he can't do:

- Watch your network at 2am on a Saturday
- Respond to ransomware inside an hour
- Tell you whether your backups actually restore (not just "look like they're running")
- Track the 50+ new vulnerabilities that drop every week
- Have an opinion on whether your cyber insurance application will pass underwriting

The thing that forces the conversation is almost never an article like this one.

It's an incident. Two days of email down during a critical week. A staff member clicking something they shouldn't have. A backup that turns out to be six months stale.

That's when the real cost of cheap IT becomes visible: lost revenue, emergency recovery rates, the quiet damage to your reputation when clients can't reach you.

The fix isn't about spending more. It's about spending differently — proactive instead of reactive.

## Post 3: AI at Work — The Conversation Nobody's Having (Friday AM)

Every business owner I talk to is in one of two camps:

Camp A: "AI is hype. We'll wait and see."
Camp B: "We need an AI strategy." (Doesn't know what that means.)

Here's the reality that lives between those two positions:

Your staff are already using ChatGPT. Right now. Today.

They're pasting customer data into it. Summarising internal documents. Drafting client emails. None of it is covered by any IT policy. None of it is visible to you.

This is not a judgement on AI. It's a data governance problem wearing a chatbot-shaped hat.

Three things every SMB should do this quarter:

1. Write a one-page AI acceptable use policy. What data can and can't go into public AI tools.
2. Understand the data boundary difference between Microsoft Copilot (respects your tenant's security) and free ChatGPT (does not). That distinction matters.
3. Ask your team what they're already using. You might be surprised by the answer.

AI isn't coming. It's already in your office, on your staff's phones, processing your data.

The question is not whether to adopt it. The question is whether you're going to manage it.
